# R5 - Trend watch and recall backtest

## What was built

`src/field_return_triage/trend/` implements PLAN.md §6 trend watch with no AI and no
network: one deterministic spike rule over the committed weekly counts, and a recall
backtest with a seeded base rate.

| Module | Public surface | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `series.py` | `load_weekly()`, `load_recalls()`, `group_weeks()`, `week_grid()`, `series_total()`, `series_sort_key()`, `series_key_dict()` | Reads the two committed CSV tables straight from `data/trend/` (the lane does not import the data lane), groups rows into series and fills gaps with zero-count weeks. Unknown columns, bad values and a duplicate `(series, week)` row fail with the file and line named. |
| `spike.py` | `detect_alerts()`, `is_spike()`, `poisson_upper_limit()`, `trailing_mean()`, `TRAILING_WEEKS`, `MIN_COUNT`, `MEAN_FLOOR`, `QUANTILE` | The rule: a count alerts when it is at least 3 **and** strictly above the Poisson 99.9 % upper limit of the trailing 26-week mean, floored at 0.5. |
| `backtest.py` | `run_backtest()`, `preceding_recall()`, `recall_index()`, `wilson_interval()`, `pick_example_series()`, `Backtest` | The 0-365 day recall join, the seeded base rate with a Wilson 95 % CI, the weak-join note and the fixed example-series rule. |
| `cli.py` | `trend_main()` | `frt trend` / `make trend`: reads the tables, writes the three JSON files, prints the alert count and the share beside the base rate. |

### The rule, exactly

- Series identity is `make + model + model_year + group` (`SeriesKey`).
- Each series is a continuous Monday grid from its first to its last row; a week with
  no row inside that span counts as zero.
- A week is evaluated only once it has 26 prior weeks. The trailing mean is those 26
  weeks divided by 26, floored at 0.5. The first 26 weeks of a series are never alerts.
- The upper limit is the smallest integer `k` with `P(X <= k) >= 0.999` for
  `X ~ Poisson(trailing_mean)`; the alert condition is `count > k`. The Poisson CDF is
  computed directly from the pmf with a mode-centred upper tail, so there is no SciPy
  dependency and no floating underflow on a large mean. `Threshold` in the alert is
  exactly that `k`, computed from the mean as stored (rounded to 6 decimals).
- Alerts are ordered by series (make, model, model year, group) then week, so the files
  are byte-identical across runs.

Worked boundaries: for a series with a trailing mean of 1, the limit is 5, so a week of
5 stays quiet and 6 alerts. For a mean floored at 0.5 the limit is 4, so 4 stays quiet
and 5 alerts; a count of 2 never alerts, whatever the limit.

### The recall join and the base rate

- An alert precedes a recall when a recall with the same make + model + model year +
  group has a report date in `[alert_week, alert_week + 365 days]`. Both edges count:
  day 0 and day 365 match; day -1 and day 366 do not. The earliest recall in the window
  is the match, and its lead time is the number of days after the alert week.
- The base rate draws **one random evaluated week of the same series per alert**
  (seed `20261031`, the project's fixed seed), and applies the identical window. Only
  weeks with a full 26-week history are eligible, so the draw is from the same set of
  weeks an alert could have come from. There are exactly as many draws as alerts.
- The base-rate share carries a Wilson 95 % CI. The backtest file always reports the
  alert share **and** the base rate beside it (`PLAN.md` §6: the comparison is the
  headline, never the raw share).
- If fewer than 20 alerts precede any recall, `alerts.weak_join` is `true` and
  `alerts.note` says plainly that the join is too weak to support a share. The alerts
  are kept, per the can-drop rule.
- The example series is the alert with the longest lead time among alerts whose series
  has at least 20 complaints in the table. Ties break on the earlier week, then the
  series key. `example_series.json` carries the full weekly grid for the chart, the
  alert and its recall.

### Output files

`make trend` writes, with sorted keys and a trailing newline:

- `results/trend/alerts.json` - the rule and parameters, alert count, series count and
  every alert (`series`, `week`, `count`, `trailing_mean`, `threshold`);
- `results/trend/backtest.json` - `alerts` (total, preceding recall, share, median lead
  days, weak-join flag and note), `base_rate` (draws, hits, share, `ci95`, seed),
  `method`, and `per_alert` rows with the matched recall id, report date and lead days;
- `results/trend/example_series.json` - `available`, `series`, `total_complaints`,
  `alert`, `recall` and `weeks` (every `{week, count}` for the chart). When no alert
  with a recall reaches 20 complaints the file says `"available": false` with a reason
  and an empty `weeks` list, so the page lane can omit the chart cleanly.

### CSV contract read by the lane

`data/trend/weekly.csv.gz`: `make,model,model_year,group,week,count`.
`data/trend/recalls.csv`: `recall_id,make,model,model_year,group,report_date`.

Both are read as UTF-8, gzip by `.gz` suffix, with dates in ISO form and weeks on a
Monday (the contract validates that). A handful of obvious aliases are accepted
(`year`/`my` for `model_year`, `week_start` for `week`, `complaints` for `count`,
`id` for `recall_id`, `date`/`rcl_date` for `report_date`, `component_group` for
`group`) so a harmless spelling difference cannot break the join. This is the schema
R1's `derive_weekly`/`derive_recalls` should write; it matches the contract field
names.

## Tests

60 new tests in `tests/trend/`, all offline:

- **spike**: known Poisson limits (mean 0.5 -> 4, 1 -> 5, 2 -> 8, 5 -> 13, 10 -> 21),
  the "smallest qualifying integer" property, no underflow at mean 1000, bad input, the
  26-week mean and its floor, the minimum-count rule, the limit boundary (equal quiet,
  one above alerts), a spike detected, a steady series quiet, no alerts before a full
  26-week history, missing weeks as zero, and the alert ordering;
- **series**: plain and gzipped CSV, aliases, unknown/missing columns, bad values with
  the line number, a non-Monday week, short rows, empty files, bad rows, duplicate
  weeks, series ordering, the zero-filled grid, empty input, totals and recall aliases;
- **backtest**: both window edges, day -1 and day 366 excluded, earliest recall wins,
  a recall never joins another series, share and median lead (odd and even), the weak
  join keeps the alerts and writes the note, a 20-alert join has no note, draws only
  evaluated weeks, base-rate determinism for a fixed seed (and a different seed draws
  different weeks), the base-rate count, known Wilson intervals and bad counts, the
  no-alert report, the example rule (longest lead with >= 20 complaints), fewer than 20
  complaints, and no recall match;
- **CLI**: all three files on a temporary gzipped table, exact content, byte-identical
  reruns, the unavailable example, clean exits for missing/bad/empty tables, and the
  `frt trend` dispatch with the path overrides.

`make test`: **308 passed** (60 new). `make lint`, `make typecheck` (strict mypy) are
clean. `make scan-forbidden` and `make scan-secrets` pass.

## Acceptance run on committed data - open

`data/trend/weekly.csv.gz` and `data/trend/recalls.csv` are **not on `main` yet**:
`data/` holds only `feedback/`, the R1 lane has not run, and the X0 snapshot folder
still lacks its `nhtsa-spec.toml`/`groups.toml`. Without R1's tables and X0's group
mapping there is no honest weekly series or recall join to compute, so **no
`results/trend/` files are committed** and the acceptance run stays open - the same
status as R3's `make index` run.

The command itself is wired and was exercised end to end against a temporary synthetic
table outside the worktree (40 series, 156 weeks, one injected spike and recall each),
using the path overrides:

```
FRT_TREND_WEEKLY=.../weekly.csv.gz FRT_TREND_RECALLS=.../recalls.csv \
FRT_TREND_RESULTS=.../results make trend
frt trend: 40 alerts in 40 series -> .../results
frt trend: 40/40 alerts precede a recall (100.0%); base rate 45.0%
          (95% CI 30.7%-60.2%), window 365 days
```

Those numbers are **synthetic acceptance evidence only**; they are not in `results/`
and must not appear on the page. As soon as R1 lands the real tables, `make trend` (and
R6's `make all`) will write the committed files; no code change is needed. `make trend`
today fails cleanly with `data/trend/weekly.csv.gz is missing; run make data first`.
